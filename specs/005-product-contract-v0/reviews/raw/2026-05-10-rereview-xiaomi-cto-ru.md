# Re-Review: CTO Value and Russian Enterprise Baseline

## Verdict

**`REVISE_BEFORE_USER_APPROVAL`**

The direction is solid and the prior review findings are substantially resolved. The P0 classification rule now works, the packet template is normative, the evidence bundle manifest exists, and the Russian enterprise baseline example (`example-local-baseline.md`) demonstrates a valid local Git plus TeamCity packet without GitHub or public SaaS dependencies.

However, three practical gaps remain that a Russian enterprise CTO would hit immediately:

1. **Evidence bundle manifest overhead**: eight required fields per entry is too heavy for self-hosted setups without dedicated evidence-management tooling.
2. **Packet generation ownership is undefined**: the contract says what the packet must contain, but not who produces it or how.
3. **Authority and decision-binding expectations are unrealistic** for the baseline profile: `PC-AUTHORITY` will always be `not_assessed`, and `PC-DECISION` binding sources assume formal infrastructure that smaller Russian enterprises may lack.

These are not direction-killers. They are practical deployment blockers that should be addressed before presenting to the CTO.

## P0 Classification Rule Assessment

The P0 classification rule now genuinely prevents substrate-only work from being presented as P0 product progress. The key mechanisms:

1. **Six required fields** (`packet_rows`, `evidence_surface`, `start_state`, `target_transition`, `buyer_effect`, `non_goal`) force specificity. A team cannot claim P0 progress by naming a row alone.

2. **Forward progress definition** is explicit: row state must move from unknown/missing to a meaningful state with evidence, or an evidence surface must become inspectable and produce retained refs, or a theater code must gain a derivation rule. Repeating `not_assessed -> not_assessed` is explicitly excluded.

3. **`buyer_effect` field** ties the feature to CTO-visible value: "what the CTO packet becomes clearer about." This prevents substrate features from being justified by internal elegance.

4. **`non_goal` field** forces the team to state what the feature still does not prove, which directly counters overclaim.

The remaining risk is narrow: a team could claim an evidence surface "became inspectable" without verifying it actually produced retained refs. But the `buyer_effect` and `non_goal` fields make this harder to sustain in a review, because the CTO will ask what became clearer-and if the answer is "nothing observable," the feature fails the buyer-effect test.

**Verdict on the rule**: It is strong enough for user approval. The risk of gaming is low and the cost of further hardening (e.g., automated forward-progress validation) is not justified at the contract stage.
